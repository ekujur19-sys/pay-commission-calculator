MACP explained: financial upgradation at 10, 20 and 30 years

The Modified Assured Career Progression Scheme makes sure an employee who is not promoted still moves up the Pay Matrix during their career. Here is who gets it, when, and how much it adds.

Last updated October 2026 · by NE Media

The short version

  • Up to three financial upgradations in a career, counted from your direct entry grade: after 10, 20 and 30 years of regular service.
  • Each upgradation moves you to the next level in the Pay Matrix. Your post and duties stay the same.
  • You also get an upgradation after 10 years of continuous service in the same level, if that comes earlier.
  • Regular promotions count towards the three. Someone promoted three times does not get MACP.
  • The benchmark is a "Very Good" grading in your Annual Performance Assessment Reports (APARs).

These are the 7th CPC terms, in force since 25 July 2016 under the Department of Personnel and Training's Office Memorandum of 28 September 2016.

When do you become eligible?

Count each upgradation from whichever comes first:

  • 10, 20 or 30 years from the date you joined in your direct entry grade, or
  • 10 years of continuous service in the level you are in now.

If a regular promotion comes before an upgradation is due, the promotion takes its place, and the next upgradation is counted from the promotion.

How pay is fixed on MACP

Pay is fixed in the same way as on promotion: one increment in your current level, then placement in the next level at the equal cell, or the next higher cell if there is no equal one. See pay fixation on promotion for the full method.

Where neighbouring levels overlap a lot, an upgradation can add just one increment. The real long-term benefit is that the higher level has more cells, and later upgradations and pay commission revisions start from a higher base. DA (60%) and HRA are added on the higher basic pay.

The "Very Good" benchmark

  • A Screening Committee checks your APARs. Under the 7th CPC the benchmark is "Very Good" for all posts (it was "Good" for many posts earlier).
  • If you do not meet the benchmark when the upgradation falls due, it is not lost for good: your case is considered again in later years, and the upgradation is granted from the date you meet it.
  • You can ask for a below-benchmark APAR to be upgraded by making a representation when it is shared with you. Do this promptly, because it affects MACP.
  • Pending disciplinary proceedings can delay MACP, in the same way as promotion.

Common questions

Does my designation change?

No. MACP is a purely financial upgradation. You keep your post, duties and seniority.

What if I am later promoted to the same level?

If the promotion post is in the level you already reached through MACP, there is no further pay fixation; only the designation changes. If it is in a higher level, pay is fixed again.

Does past service count?

Regular service counts from your direct entry grade. Ad hoc, contract or casual service before regular appointment generally does not. Service in another Central Government department can count in some cases, such as technical resignation to join a new post; check with your office.

When is it paid?

From the date it falls due, once the Screening Committee approves it. Committees usually meet in the first month of each half-year, so arrears are often paid for a few months at once.

What will the 8th Pay Commission change?

The 8th CPC's terms of reference allow it to review career progression. Employee organisations have asked for more upgradations and a lower benchmark. Nothing has been decided; follow the latest news.

Sources

Department of Personnel and Training Office Memorandum No. 35034/3/2015-Estt.(D) of 28 September 2016 (MACPS under the 7th CPC), and Rule 13 of the Central Civil Services (Revised Pay) Rules, 2016 for pay fixation. Orders are published on the DoPT website. Defence forces and some organisations have their own versions of the scheme.
