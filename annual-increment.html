Annual increment rules: 1 January and 1 July

How much your yearly increment is, when you get it, what can delay it, and what it adds to your take-home pay, with examples from the 7th CPC Pay Matrix.

Last updated October 2026 · by NE Media

The short version

  • Each year you move one cell down your level in the Pay Matrix. Each cell is about 3% higher than the one above it, rounded to the nearest ₹100.
  • There are two increment dates: 1 January and 1 July. Each employee has one of them, depending on when they joined, were promoted or got a financial upgradation.
  • You need at least six months of qualifying service in the same level before your increment date.
  • DA, HRA and other allowances that are a percentage of basic pay go up automatically with the increment.

How much is the increment?

Under the 7th CPC there is no separate "increment amount". The Pay Matrix already contains it: every cell in a level is the previous cell plus 3%, rounded to the nearest hundred rupees. Your increment is simply the difference between your cell and the next one.

Because the step is a percentage, it grows as you move down a level: at Level 6 the step from ₹50,500 to ₹52,000 is ₹1,500, not ₹1,100. The full list of cells for every level is on the Pay Matrix page.

What it adds to your pay

Example: Level 6, basic pay ₹35,400, posted in an X-class city, DA 60%.

If you are on NPS, your 10% contribution also rises by ₹176 (10% of the extra basic pay and DA), so the change in take-home pay is a little smaller. Transport allowance does not change with an increment, only with DA.

1 January or 1 July: which is yours?

Since 2016 there have been two increment dates. Which one applies depends on the date you joined, or the date of your last promotion or financial upgradation (MACP):

You joined, were promoted or upgraded between…Your increment date
2 January and 1 July1 January of the following year, and every 1 January after that
2 July and 1 January1 July of the following year, and every 1 July after that
  • Joined on 15 March 2025: first increment on 1 January 2026, then every 1 January.
  • Joined on 10 August 2025: first increment on 1 July 2026, then every 1 July.
  • Promoted on 1 October 2026: the date moves to 1 July. The first increment in the new level is due on 1 July 2027.

The rule exists so that everyone has completed at least six months in the level before the first increment. Before 2016 there was a single date, 1 July, and people who joined in, say, February waited up to 17 months.

What can delay or stop an increment

  • Extraordinary leave (EOL) without a medical certificate does not count as service for increments, so your increment date moves forward by the period of that leave. EOL on medical grounds or for higher studies may count, depending on the sanction.
  • Penalty of withholding of increments under the conduct and discipline rules stops increments for the period in the order.
  • Probation not completed in some services: the increment can be held until probation is cleared.
  • Earned leave, half pay leave, commuted leave and child care leave do count; they do not delay your increment.

Reaching the top of your level

Each level has a fixed number of cells (40 for Levels 1 to 10, fewer for higher levels). Under the 7th CPC Pay Rules, an employee who reaches the last cell of a level is placed in the next higher level one year later, so increments do not stop completely. In practice most employees get a promotion or a MACP upgradation long before they reach the end of a level.

Retiring on 30 June or 31 December: the notional increment

For years, an employee who retired on 30 June missed the increment due the very next day, 1 July, and their pension was worked out on the lower basic pay. After a Supreme Court ruling on this question, the Department of Personnel and Training issued orders (OM of 20 May 2025, following an earlier OM of 14 October 2024) allowing a notional increment on 1 July or 1 January for employees who retire the day before.

  • It applies to employees who retire on superannuation after completing the qualifying period, with satisfactory work and conduct.
  • It is counted only for working out pension, not for gratuity, leave encashment or commutation.
  • The higher pension is payable from 1 May 2023.
  • Employees who take voluntary retirement or leave service for other reasons before their increment date do not get it.

Example: Level 7, last basic pay ₹60,400 on 30 June. With the notional increment pension is worked out on ₹62,200, so basic pension becomes ₹31,100 instead of ₹30,200, ₹900 a month more before Dearness Relief. Try it in the pension calculator.

Increments and the 8th Pay Commission

  • When the 8th CPC pay is fixed, your basic pay on the changeover date is multiplied by the fitment factor and placed in the new Pay Matrix. Each increment you get before then raises the starting point for that calculation. Under the 7th CPC, the first increment in the new matrix came on 1 July 2016, six months after the 1 January 2016 changeover.
  • Employee organisations have asked the 8th CPC to raise the increment rate from 3% to 6% a year. This is a demand, not a decision; see the latest news.
  • You can see how an increment changes your estimated 8th CPC pay by choosing the next cell in the 8th Pay calculator.

Sources

Increment rules are in Rule 10 of the Central Civil Services (Revised Pay) Rules, 2016, as amended to provide two increment dates; the Pay Matrix is in the Cabinet decisions on the 7th CPC (PDF). Notional increment orders are published by the Department of Personnel and Training. Your office's pay bill section has the final word on your own increment date.
