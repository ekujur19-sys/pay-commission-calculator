DA hike history under the 7th Pay Commission

Every Dearness Allowance rate from the start of the 7th CPC in January 2016 to today, and what happens to DA when the 8th Pay Commission starts.

Last updated October 2026 · by NE Media

What is Dearness Allowance?

Dearness Allowance (DA) protects salaries against inflation. It is a percentage of basic pay, revised twice a year, with effect from 1 January and 1 July. The rate follows the All-India Consumer Price Index for Industrial Workers (AICPI-IW). Revisions are usually announced a few months after the date they take effect, and the months in between are paid as DA arrears. Pensioners receive the same rate as Dearness Relief (DR).

How the DA rate is worked out

DA is not decided at will: it follows a formula set by the 7th CPC and accepted by the Government.

DA % = (average AICPI-IW for the last 12 months − 261.42) ÷ 261.42 × 100

  • AICPI-IW is the All-India Consumer Price Index for Industrial Workers, published every month by the Labour Bureau.
  • 261.42 is the average index for 2015 (base 2001 = 100), the level built into the 7th CPC pay. Since September 2020 the index uses base 2016 = 100, so the new figures are multiplied by the linking factor of 2.88 before using the formula.
  • For the DA from 1 January, the average of the 12 months ending in December is used; for 1 July, the 12 months ending in June.
  • The result is rounded down to a whole number. If it works out at 62.9%, the new DA is 62%.

This is why the expected DA hike is often known weeks before the Cabinet decision: once the last month's index is out, anyone can work it out. The Cabinet still has to approve it before it is paid.

When DA hikes are announced

The Cabinet usually approves the January DA in March or April and the July DA in September or October, often just before Holi and Diwali. The order is then issued by the Department of Expenditure, and the arrears for the months already passed are paid with the next salary. The 1 January 2026 increase, for example, was approved on 18 April 2026 and paid with arrears from January.

How DA affects other allowances

  • HRA: House Rent Allowance rates step up when DA crosses 25% and again at 50%. See the HRA guide.
  • Transport Allowance: DA is also paid on top of transport allowance. See transport allowance rates.
  • Allowances that rose by 25% when DA reached 50% on 1 January 2024: Children Education Allowance and hostel subsidy, dress allowance, the gratuity ceiling (from ₹20 lakh to ₹25 lakh), and several special allowances.
  • Leave encashment and gratuity are worked out on basic pay plus DA, so a DA increase also raises these at retirement.

Will DA be merged with basic pay?

When DA crossed 50% in 2024, many reports suggested that half of it would be merged into basic pay, as happened in 2004. The Government told Parliament that there was no such proposal; DA continues to be paid separately until the 8th CPC pay is fixed.

What happens to DA at the 8th Pay Commission?

When a new pay commission starts, the DA earned so far is folded into the new basic pay through the fitment factor, and DA restarts at 0%. It then begins rising again every January and July. That is why the 8th CPC calculator starts 8th CPC DA at 0% by default.

Because 8th CPC pay is expected to apply from 1 January 2026, but will be paid only after the Commission reports, the 7th CPC DA hikes announced in the meantime (60% from January 2026, and the expected July 2026 increase) will be paid as usual and later adjusted in the arrears calculation.
