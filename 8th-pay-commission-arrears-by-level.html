8th Pay Commission arrears: how much could land in your account?

At Level 6, the arrears could be more than ₹6 lakh. At Level 10, over ₹10 lakh. We worked out the numbers for every level, so you can see what is at stake while the Commission writes its report.

Analysis · 23 November 2026 · by NE Media

These are estimates, not announcements. The fitment factor, the effective date and the payment date have not been decided. The figures below show what the arrears would be under clearly stated assumptions, using the same method as our arrears calculator.

Why a lump sum is likely

Pay commissions usually count from a fixed date, but are paid only after the report is accepted. The 8th CPC is widely expected to count from 1 January 2026. Its report is due by about May 2027, and the Government then needs a few months to decide and issue orders. So new pay is most likely to start somewhere in 2027 or early 2028.

For every month in between, you are owed the difference between the new pay and what you were actually paid. That difference, added up, is your arrears. The 7th CPC did exactly this: it counted from January 2016, was approved in June 2016, and arrears were paid in one go.

Going from 1.92 to 2.28 roughly doubles the arrears, and 2.86 nearly doubles them again. That is because the arrears come only from the part of the new pay above today's basic pay plus DA: with DA at 60% in January 2026, a factor of 1.92 leaves a gap of only 0.32 times basic pay, while 2.28 leaves 0.68. The fitment factor guide explains why.

How we worked it out

  • 8th CPC pay counts from 1 January 2026. New basic pay = basic pay on that date × fitment factor, rounded to ₹100.
  • 7th CPC DA as actually paid: 60% from January 2026 and 63% from July 2026, then an assumed 3% rise every January and July.
  • 8th CPC DA starts at 0% and also rises 3% every six months.
  • Annual increment every July on both sides (3%).
  • Basic pay and DA only. Under the 7th CPC, HRA and other allowances were revised later without arrears; if HRA arrears are paid this time, the totals would be higher.

What you will actually receive

  • NPS or UPS: 10% of the extra basic pay and DA goes to your pension account, so roughly 10% less comes to your bank. The Government adds its 14% on top.
  • Income tax: arrears are taxed in the year they are paid. A big lump sum can push you into a higher slab, but relief for arrears (formerly Section 89) lets you pay tax as if the money had come in the years it belongs to. At Level 6 with 24 months of arrears, our calculator estimates the relief at about ₹74,000. Read how arrears are taxed.
  • Instalments are possible. The 6th CPC paid arrears in two parts (40% and 60%); the 7th CPC paid in one go.

Five things to do now

  1. Keep your pay slips from January 2026 onwards. Arrears are worked out month by month, and you will want to check them.
  2. Note your increment, promotion and MACP dates. Each changes the arrears. See increments and pay fixation on promotion.
  3. Do not count the money yet. Until the fitment factor is known, plan with the lowest figure above, not the highest.
  4. Plan for tax. Check whether the old or new regime suits you in the year of payment, and remember to file Form 10E for the relief.
  5. Run your own numbers in the arrears calculator: pick your level, basic pay, fitment factor and the month new pay starts.
