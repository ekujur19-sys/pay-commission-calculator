8th Pay Commission: latest news

Key developments on the 8th Central Pay Commission and related pay decisions, newest first, with a link to the official source for each.

Last updated 23 November 2026 · by NE Media

Featured analysis 8th Pay Commission arrears: how much could land in your account? Over ₹6 lakh at Level 6, over ₹10 lakh at Level 10. We worked out the arrears for every level at fitment factors of 1.92, 2.28 and 2.86. Read the level-by-level estimates →

Latest: the Finance Ministry has issued the order for 63% DA from 1 July 2026, so offices can now pay the higher DA with arrears from July. Next: the price index points to DA of about 66–67% from January 2027. Check your new take-home pay in the calculator.

Coming up

  • Nov – Dec 2026Payment of the 63% DA and the July–October arrears, with November or December salary in most offices (not before October salary is paid, as the order requires).
  • To be announcedThe Commission’s next city visit. No visit after Mumbai has been notified as of 23 November.
  • Jan 2027ExpectedDA from 1 January 2027, usually announced around March. Price index data up to September points to about 66–67%; the October to December figures will settle it.
  • By about May 2027ExpectedThe Commission’s report is due within 18 months of its constitution on 3 November 2025.

Timeline

  1. 23 November 2026

    AnalysisHow big could 8th CPC arrears be?

    If 8th CPC pay counts from January 2026 and is paid from January 2028, arrears on basic pay and DA at a fitment factor of 2.28 would be about ₹3.3 lakh at Level 1, ₹6.4 lakh at Level 6 and ₹10.2 lakh at Level 10. At 1.92 they would be roughly half that.

    Read the full estimates for every level

  2. 30 October 2026

    DAPrice index points to about 66–67% DA from January 2027

    The All-India Consumer Price Index for Industrial Workers rose 1.1 points to 155.5 in September 2026. If it stays around that level for October to December, the DA formula gives about 67% from 1 January 2027, a rise of 4%; a smaller rise to 66% is also possible. The Cabinet decides the actual rate, usually around March.

    Source: Labour Bureau data, reported by Upstox · How the DA formula works

  3. 29 October 2026

    OfficialFinance Ministry issues the 63% DA order

    The Department of Expenditure’s Office Memorandum No. 1/2(i)/2026-E.II(B) revises DA from 60% to 63% of basic pay from 1 July 2026. The arrears cannot be paid before the October 2026 salary. Separate orders follow for the armed forces and the railways, and pensioners get the same 63% as Dearness Relief.

    Source: Department of Expenditure, reported by GConnect and StaffNews

  4. 3 November 2026

    OfficialThe Commission completes one year

    One year after its constitution, the Commission has finished its round of questionnaires and memoranda and held meetings in 15 places, including three rounds in Delhi. Nothing on pay has been decided yet: the fitment factor, new Pay Matrix, allowances and the date of implementation all await the report, due by about May 2027.

    Source: 8th CPC – What’s New · 8th Pay Commission guide

  5. 28 October 2026

    DADA raised to 63% from 1 July 2026

    The Union Cabinet approved a 3% increase in Dearness Allowance and Dearness Relief, from 60% to 63% of basic pay and pension, with effect from 1 July 2026. The Government puts the cost at ₹10,016.10 crore a year, for about 48.54 lakh employees and 68.71 lakh pensioners. The approval came after the Cabinet meetings of 14 and 21 October passed without it.

    What it means: at Level 6 entry pay (₹35,400), DA goes up by ₹1,062 a month, and the arrears for July to October come to ₹4,248. Transport allowance rises too, because DA is paid on it. See the table for every level.

    Source: Cabinet release, 28 Oct 2026 · Business Standard

  6. 22–23 October 2026

    OfficialCommission visits Mumbai

    The Commission held its scheduled meetings in Mumbai with associations and unions that had requested appointments by 10 October. It is the latest of the field visits that began in Dehradun in April 2026. No further visit has been announced yet.

    Source: 8th CPC – What’s New (notice of 21 September 2026)

  7. 7–8 October 2026

    OfficialCommission meets stakeholders in Bengaluru

    The Commission held its scheduled meetings with associations and unions in Bengaluru. Mumbai (22–23 October) followed.

    Source: 8th CPC – What’s New

  8. 21 September 2026

    OfficialCommission to visit Mumbai on 22–23 October

    The 8th CPC announced a visit to Mumbai for meetings with associations and unions, with requests due by 10 October 2026.

    Source: 8th CPC – What’s New

  9. 24 August 2026

    OfficialBengaluru visit on 7–8 October announced

    Requests for meetings during the Bengaluru visit were due by 18 September 2026.

    Source: 8th CPC – What’s New

  10. August – September 2026

    OfficialState visits: Jaipur, Chennai, Puducherry and Chandigarh

    After meetings in Delhi on 7 and 10 August, the Commission visited Jaipur (31 August – 1 September), Chennai (7–8 September), Puducherry (9 September) and Chandigarh (16–18 September). On 11 August it also partly modified its guidelines for engaging consultants.

    Source: 8th CPC – What’s New

  11. June – July 2026

    OfficialVisits to Ladakh, Lucknow, Bhubaneswar and Kolkata

    The Commission met stakeholders in Ladakh (8 June), Lucknow (22–23 June), Bhubaneswar (6–7 July) and Kolkata (9–10 July).

    Source: 8th CPC – What’s New

  12. 29 May 2026

    OfficialDeadline for memorandum responses extended to 15 June 2026

    The second extension (the first, on 29 April, had moved it to 31 May) gave ministries, associations and other stakeholders more time to send their memoranda and responses.

    Source: 8th CPC – What’s New

  13. April – June 2026

    OfficialFirst round of visits: Dehradun, Delhi, Pune, Hyderabad, Srinagar

    The Commission’s field visits began in Dehradun (24 April), followed by meetings in Delhi (28–30 April and 13–14 May), Pune (4–5 May), Hyderabad (18–19 May) and Srinagar (1–4 June).

    Source: 8th CPC – What’s New

  14. 18 April 2026

    DADA raised to 60% from 1 January 2026

    The Union Cabinet approved a 2% increase in Dearness Allowance and Dearness Relief, from 58% to 60% of basic pay and pension, with effect from 1 January 2026.

    Source: Cabinet release, 18 Apr 2026 · DA order, 22 Apr 2026 (PDF) · DA history

  15. 14 April 2026

    DemandStaff Side submits its memorandum

    The Staff Side of the National Council (JCM), which represents employees, asked the Commission for a fitment factor of 3.833, minimum basic pay of about ₹69,000 (now ₹18,000), 6% annual increments and HRA linked to DA. These are the employees’ demands, not decisions.

    Source: Business Today, 15 Apr 2026 · Fitment factor explained

  16. Early 2026

    OfficialQuestionnaire and memorandum submissions open

    The Commission invited views through a questionnaire on its website (last date extended to 31 March 2026), and on 5 March 2026 invited memoranda from associations and unions of employees and pensioners, organisations and individuals. It also issued guidelines for engaging consultants (10 April 2026).

    Source: 8th CPC Questionnaire · Memorandum submission · Consultant circulars

  17. 3 November 2025

    Official8th Pay Commission formally constituted

    The Government notified the constitution of the Eighth Central Pay Commission in the Gazette. The Commission is based at Chanderlok Building, Janpath, New Delhi.

    Source: Gazette notification, 3 Nov 2025 (PDF)

  18. 28 October 2025

    OfficialCabinet approves terms of reference and members

    Justice Ranjana Prakash Desai was named Chairperson, Prof. Pulak Ghosh as part-time Member and Shri Pankaj Jain as Member-Secretary. The Commission is to report within 18 months, and its recommendations are normally expected to take effect from 1 January 2026.

    Source: Cabinet release, 28 Oct 2025 · 8th CPC – Terms of Reference

  19. 1 October 2025

    DADA raised to 58% from 1 July 2025

    A 3% increase in DA and DR, from 55% to 58%.

    Source: Cabinet release, 1 Oct 2025

  20. January 2025

    OfficialCabinet approves setting up the 8th Pay Commission

    The Union Cabinet approved the formation of the 8th Central Pay Commission to revise the pay and pensions of Central Government employees and pensioners.

    Source: Cabinet release, 28 Oct 2025 (records the January 2025 announcement)

What has not been decided yet

The fitment factor, the new Pay Matrix, allowances, pension revision and the date new pay is actually paid are all still open. Any figures you see reported for these are expectations, not decisions.
