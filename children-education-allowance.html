Children Education Allowance and hostel subsidy

A fixed yearly payment for the schooling of your two eldest children, whatever the school actually charges. Here are the current rates, who can claim, and the paperwork.

Last updated October 2026 · by NE Media

Current rates

ItemPer monthPer year
Children Education Allowance (CEA), per child₹2,812.50₹33,750
Hostel subsidy, per child₹8,437.50₹1,01,250
CEA for a child with a disability (double rate)₹5,625₹67,500

These rates apply from 1 January 2024. The 7th CPC set CEA at ₹2,250 and hostel subsidy at ₹6,750 a month, and both rise by 25% each time DA crosses another 50%. DA reached 50% on 1 January 2024, which triggered the increase. The next rise would come only if DA reached 100% under the 7th CPC, which will not happen before the 8th CPC.

Example: two children in school, no hostel: 2 × ₹33,750 = ₹67,500 a year.

Who is eligible?

  • Central Government employees, for their two eldest surviving children. If the second child is followed by twins (or a multiple birth), the twins together count, so CEA can be paid for three.
  • Classes from nursery to class 12, including classes 11 and 12 in junior colleges and the first two years of a diploma or certificate course from a polytechnic or ITI taken after class 10.
  • The school must be recognised by the Central or State Government, a Board or a University.
  • If both parents are Government employees, only one of them can claim for a child.
  • If the employee dies in service, CEA continues to be paid for the children under the rules.

Hostel subsidy

  • Paid when the child lives in the hostel of a residential school at least 50 km from your residence, for example because of a transfer.
  • CEA and hostel subsidy can be claimed for the same child.
  • A Department of Personnel and Training order of 30 June 2026 clarified how hostel subsidy claims are to be reimbursed. If you claim hostel subsidy, read it or ask your office before filing.

Children with disabilities

For a child with a disability of 40% or more, CEA is paid at double the normal rate (₹5,625 a month), and the age limit for school is relaxed. Women employees with disabilities also get a separate child care special allowance of ₹3,750 a month (from 1 January 2024) for children up to two years old.

How to claim

  1. Claim once a year, after the academic year ends.
  2. Attach a certificate from the head of the school that the child studied there during the year, or a self-attested copy of the report card, or the fee receipt or e-receipt for the year.
  3. For hostel subsidy, the certificate must also show that the child stayed in the hostel, with the period.
  4. The fixed amount is paid regardless of the actual fees, so you do not need to submit fee details beyond the proof of study.

Many offices allow claims through the employee self-service section of the pay system. Do not leave it too long: offices can refuse very old claims.

Income tax on CEA

  • Old tax regime, from 1 April 2026: the Income-tax Rules, 2026 (notified on 20 March 2026) raised the exempt limit from ₹100 to ₹3,000 a month for education and from ₹300 to ₹9,000 a month for hostel, for up to two children. Since CEA is ₹2,812.50 and hostel subsidy ₹8,437.50, both are now fully tax-free in the old regime.
  • New tax regime: the whole allowance is taxable as salary.
  • For two children, that is ₹67,500 a year of tax-free income in the old regime (more with hostel subsidy). It can tip the balance towards the old regime for some employees. See old vs new tax regime.

CEA and the 8th Pay Commission

Each pay commission resets fixed allowances like CEA. When the 8th CPC pay comes in and DA restarts from 0%, a new base amount for CEA and hostel subsidy is expected to be set, with a new rule for increases. Until then, the rates above continue. Follow the latest news.

Sources

Department of Personnel and Training orders on Children Education Allowance under the 7th CPC (17 July 2018 and later), the order of April 2024 raising the rates by 25% after DA reached 50%, and the order of 30 June 2026 on hostel subsidy, on the DoPT website. Income tax exemption limits: Income-tax Rules, 2026 (notification G.S.R. 198(E) of 20 March 2026), on the Income Tax Department website.
