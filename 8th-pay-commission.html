8th Pay Commission: status, timeline and expected salary

What the 8th Central Pay Commission is, where things stand, when new pay may start, and what starting basic pay could look like at each level.

Last updated October 2026 · by NE Media

What is the 8th Pay Commission?

A Central Pay Commission is a body the Government of India sets up, usually once every ten years, to review the pay, allowances and pensions of Central Government employees and pensioners. Its recommendations become the new pay structure once the Government accepts them.

The 7th Pay Commission’s pay structure took effect on 1 January 2016. Following the ten-year pattern, the 8th Pay Commission’s recommendations are widely expected to count from 1 January 2026, even if the new pay is actually paid later, with arrears for the gap.

Where things stand

The Union Government announced the 8th Pay Commission in January 2025 and approved its terms of reference in late 2025, with Justice Ranjana Prakash Desai (retired) named as its chair. The Commission was given about 18 months to submit its report, which points to a report in 2027.

After the report, the Government decides what to accept and issues orders. For the 7th CPC, the report came in November 2015 and the new pay was approved in June 2016, counted from January 2016, with arrears paid for the months in between. A similar gap is likely this time.

What the Commission has been asked to consider

Its terms of reference ask it to recommend pay, allowances and pensions while keeping in mind, among other things:

  • the economic conditions in the country and the need for fiscal prudence;
  • the need to keep enough resources for development spending and welfare;
  • the unfunded cost of non-contributory pension schemes (the old pension scheme);
  • the likely impact on State Government finances, since many States adopt the Central pay structure with changes;
  • pay, benefits and working conditions in Central public sector undertakings and the private sector.

Full text: 8th CPC terms of reference.

How the Commission is working

Since early 2026 the Commission has collected views through an online questionnaire and memoranda from ministries, employee associations and others (the deadline was extended to 15 June 2026). Since May 2026 it has been visiting cities to meet associations and unions: Delhi, Ladakh, Lucknow, Bhubaneswar, Kolkata, Jaipur, Chennai, Puducherry, Chandigarh, Bengaluru and Mumbai so far. Dates are on our news page.

What employees have asked for

The Staff Side of the National Council (JCM), which represents Central Government employees, submitted its memorandum in April 2026. Its main demands:

  • a fitment factor of 3.833, with minimum basic pay of about ₹69,000 (now ₹18,000);
  • annual increments of 6% instead of 3% (see annual increment rules);
  • two increments on promotion, and HRA linked to DA.

These are demands. The Government's own position will appear only after the Commission reports. In past commissions, the final fitment factor was well below the figures employees asked for.

Nothing about 8th CPC pay has been decided yet. The fitment factor, Pay Matrix, allowances and effective date will only be known when the Government accepts the report. Figures below and in the calculator are estimates.

Why take-home pay rises less than basic pay

Basic pay jumps a lot, but Dearness Allowance (DA) is normally reset to 0% when a new pay commission starts, because the old DA is built into the new basic pay. In January 2026, 7th CPC DA was 60%. So a fitment factor of 2.28 raises basic pay by 128%, while basic pay plus DA rises by a much smaller share. HRA rates can also change when DA resets. See the fitment factor guide for a worked example.

What happens to arrears?

If the 8th CPC counts from 1 January 2026 but the new pay is only paid later, you receive the difference for the months in between as arrears. Arrears are taxable in the year you receive them, but you can reduce the extra tax with relief under Section 89.

What it means for pensioners

Pensioners not on NPS are covered too. Under the 7th CPC, basic pension was multiplied by the same 2.57 factor and Dearness Relief restarted at 0%. If the 8th CPC follows that pattern, a pensioner with basic pension ₹20,000 would get ₹45,600 at 2.28, against ₹32,000 now (with 60% DR). The commuted part of pension is not revised. Try it in the pension calculator and read the pension rules guide.

Quick answers

When will I get the new salary?

Not before the report (due by about May 2027) is accepted and the new pay rules are notified. Payment during 2027-28 is the most commonly discussed scenario, with arrears from 1 January 2026 if that remains the effective date.

Will DA be merged into basic pay before that?

The Government has said there is no such proposal. 7th CPC DA continues to be revised every six months until the new pay comes in. See the DA history.

Will HRA and other allowances change?

Very likely. The 7th CPC revised HRA rates, abolished or merged many small allowances, and changed others. Under the 7th CPC the revised allowances came only from 1 July 2017, eighteen months after the new pay, without arrears.

Does the 8th CPC apply to State Government employees?

No. It covers Central Government employees and pensioners. States set up their own pay commissions or adopt the Central recommendations, with changes, at their own pace.
