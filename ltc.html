LTC rules: block 2026–2029 explained

Leave Travel Concession pays for you and your family to travel to your home town, or anywhere in India, a few times every four years. Here is how the blocks work, who can travel, and how to claim.

Last updated October 2026 · by NE Media

The short version

  • LTC works in four-year blocks. The current block is 2026–2029, made of two two-year blocks: 2026–2027 and 2028–2029.
  • In each two-year block you can visit your home town once. Or, in the four-year block, you can swap one home town visit for one journey to anywhere in India.
  • LTC pays the fare for the shortest direct route, in your entitled class. It does not pay for hotels, food or local sightseeing.
  • You can encash 10 days of earned leave with each LTC, up to 60 days in your career.

How many journeys can you make?

EmployeeIn each four-year block
Regular employeesHome town twice (once in each two-year block), or home town once and anywhere in India once
New recruits, first two blocks of service (eight years)Home town three times and anywhere in India once, in each four-year block
Employees whose home town and place of posting are the sameAnywhere in India once in each four-year block (no home town LTC)

The new recruit facility applies to your first appointment in Central Government service. It runs from the date of joining for eight years, not to the fixed calendar blocks.

Carrying LTC forward

If you do not use an LTC within its block, you can use it in the first year of the next block. For example, a home town LTC of the 2026–2027 block can still be used in 2028, and an all-India LTC of the 2022–2025 block could be used during 2026.

Who counts as family?

  • Your spouse (only one) and two children, including step-children and adopted children. The limit of two does not apply to children born before October 1998, or where a second birth produced twins.
  • Your parents and step-mother, if they live with you and depend on you. Unmarried, divorced or widowed sisters and minor brothers who live with you and depend on you also count.
  • "Dependent" means a monthly income, including pension and Dearness Relief, below a limit set by the Government (₹9,000 plus DR on it, linked to the minimum pension).

Family members do not have to travel with you or at the same time, as long as each journey is completed within the same block.

Which class can you travel in?

LTC travel class follows your pay level, broadly in line with your entitlement for official tours:

  • Level 14 and above: business or club class by air.
  • Levels 9 to 13: economy class by air.
  • Levels 6 to 8: air travel is allowed for official tours but not for LTC, except under the special schemes below. Rail travel in AC classes.
  • Level 5 and below: rail travel in the class of your entitlement.

Air tickets must be bought directly from airlines or through the authorised agents (Balmer Lawrie, Ashok Travels and Tours, IRCTC), and the fare is reimbursed up to the LTC-80 fare of Air India or the actual fare, whichever is lower. If you travel in a higher class than your entitlement, you get the fare of your entitled class only. If you travel by your own vehicle or taxi, you get the rail fare of your entitled class for the shortest route.

Special schemes: North East, Jammu and Kashmir, Ladakh, Andaman and Nicobar

For several years the Government has allowed all employees, including those not otherwise entitled to fly, to travel by economy air on LTC to these regions, by converting a home town LTC. The scheme is extended for limited periods, so check the latest DoPT order before booking.

Encashing leave with LTC

  • Up to 10 days of earned leave can be encashed each time you avail LTC, to a maximum of 60 days in your career.
  • The amount is (basic pay + DA) ÷ 30 per day. At basic pay ₹56,100 and 60% DA, 10 days is worth ₹29,920.
  • The encashed days are not counted against the 300 days you can encash at retirement. See the leave rules guide.
  • Unlike the fare, leave encashed with LTC is taxable as salary.

Advance and claims

  • You can draw an advance of up to 90% of the estimated fare for yourself and your family. It is drawn shortly before the journey, and you must show the tickets to your office within a few days of drawing it.
  • If you took an advance, the claim must be submitted within one month of returning. Otherwise, within three months. A late claim can be refused and the advance recovered in one go.
  • Keep the tickets or boarding passes and, for air travel, the booking invoice from the authorised source.
  • You must be on leave (earned leave, half pay leave, casual leave or others) during the journey. LTC can be combined with holidays.

Income tax on LTC

The fare reimbursed under LTC is exempt from income tax for two journeys in a four-year block (the current income tax block is 2026–2029), but only in the old tax regime. In the new regime it is taxable. Only the fare for travel within India is exempt; leave encashment is not. See old vs new tax regime.

Sources

Central Civil Services (Leave Travel Concession) Rules, 1988, as amended, and Department of Personnel and Training orders on LTC entitlements under the 7th CPC (19 September 2017 and later clarifications) and on the special air travel schemes, published on the DoPT website. Fare rules change often; check the current order before you book.
