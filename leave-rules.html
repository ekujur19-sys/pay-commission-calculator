Leave rules for Central Government employees

How much leave of each kind you get, how it is credited, what it pays, and how unused leave turns into cash at retirement.

Last updated October 2026 · by NE Media

Earned leave (EL)

  • Credited in advance, 15 days on 1 January and 15 days on 1 July.
  • In the half-year you join, you get 2½ days for each completed month of service.
  • Your balance can be carried forward up to 300 days. When a credit would take you past 300, the extra is kept separately and used first in that half-year; whatever is unused by the end of the half-year lapses.
  • Extraordinary leave or unauthorised absence reduces the next credit by one-tenth of those days (up to 15 days).
  • You can take up to 180 days of EL at a time.

Half pay leave and commuted leave

  • HPL: 10 days credited on 1 January and 10 days on 1 July, i.e. 20 days for each completed year. It has no upper limit. Leave salary is half of basic pay, plus DA on that half.
  • Commuted leave converts HPL into full-pay leave: each day uses two days of HPL. It needs a medical certificate, except in some cases such as up to 60 days following maternity leave or for an approved course of study.
  • HPL also counts towards leave encashment at retirement if your EL balance is below 300 days (see below).

Leave for parents

Maternity leave

180 days on full pay for a woman employee with fewer than two surviving children. It can be combined with other leave due. Separate leave of up to 45 days in the whole career is available in case of miscarriage or abortion, on medical certificate.

Paternity leave

15 days for a male employee with fewer than two surviving children, to be taken within six months of the child's birth (or of adoption, for paternity leave on adoption). It lapses if not taken within that time.

Child care leave (CCL)

  • For women employees and for single male parents (unmarried, widowed or divorced).
  • 730 days in the whole career, to look after up to two eldest children until they turn 18. There is no age limit for a child with a disability of 40% or more.
  • Leave salary is 100% for the first 365 days and 80% for the next 365 days.
  • It can be taken in spells of at least 5 days, normally up to three spells a year (six for single women employees).
  • CCL is not debited against your EL or HPL, so it does not reduce leave encashment.

Child adoption leave

180 days for a woman employee with fewer than two surviving children who adopts a child up to one year old.

Leave encashment

With LTC

You can encash 10 days of EL each time you avail LTC, up to 60 days in your career. These days are not deducted from the 300-day limit for encashment at retirement. See the LTC guide.

At retirement

  • Unused EL and HPL together, up to 300 days, are paid as a lump sum.
  • EL is paid at (basic pay + DA) ÷ 30 per day.
  • HPL is paid at half that rate, and only to make up the shortfall when EL is below 300 days.
  • For Government employees, leave encashment at retirement is fully exempt from income tax.

Example: last basic pay ₹78,800, DA 60%, so basic + DA is ₹1,26,080. With 300 days of EL, leave encashment is ₹1,26,080 ÷ 30 × 300 = ₹12,60,800. With 240 days of EL and 100 days of HPL, it is 240 days at the full rate (₹10,08,640) plus 60 days of HPL at half rate (₹1,26,080), a total of ₹11,34,720. Work out your own figure with the gratuity and leave encashment calculator.

Casual leave and holidays

Casual leave (8 days a year) and restricted holidays (2 a year) are not "leave" under the CCS (Leave) Rules. They are not credited in advance, cannot be carried forward and cannot be encashed. Casual leave can be combined with holidays but not normally with earned leave.

Leave and the 8th Pay Commission

The 7th CPC made only small changes to leave rules, mainly to child care leave. The 8th CPC can recommend changes to leave as part of its review of service conditions; employee organisations have asked for more earned leave and a higher encashment limit. Nothing has been decided. Note that when DA resets to 0% under new pay, the per-day rate for leave encashment depends on the new basic pay plus the new DA.

Sources

Central Civil Services (Leave) Rules, 1972, as amended, and Department of Personnel and Training orders on child care leave and casual leave, available on the DoPT website. Some departments, the railways and the defence services have their own leave rules; check with your office.
